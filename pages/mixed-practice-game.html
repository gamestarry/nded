    How Adaptive Rounds Work

    10 facts per round: Practice a mixed set from the 2–12 times tables.

    Mistakes come back: Any fact that is wrong or unanswered when Check is pressed is carried into the next round.

    Master it and move on: Get a review fact right when you check the next round and it leaves the review pool, making room for new facts.

    Mixed Multiplication Practice from 2 to 12

    Knowing a times table in order is useful, but fluent recall means recognizing a multiplication fact without knowing which table is coming next. This game mixes facts from the 2 through 12 times tables in short, focused rounds.

    🎯 Mistakes Become Your Next Practice

    Each round has only 10 questions, and the game remembers what needs another look.

    1. Solve 10 Mixed Facts

    The questions are drawn from multiplication facts with tables 2–12, so students cannot rely on a predictable sequence.

    2. See Exactly What Was Missed

    Press Check whenever you are ready. Wrong answers and unanswered facts are listed clearly for review.

    3. Meet Those Facts Again

    Missed or unanswered facts are mixed into the next set of 10. Once a review fact is correct when the next round is checked, it leaves the review pool.

    Why Mixed Practice Matters

    Working through one table at a time helps students notice patterns and build confidence. Mixed practice adds the next skill: retrieving a fact when there is no sequence to guide the answer.

    • Builds multiplication fact fluency across tables
    • Revisits difficult facts without turning the whole session into drill
    • Keeps rounds short enough for classroom warm-ups or independent practice
    • Uses mistakes as useful information about what to practice next

    What Facts Are Included?

    Questions use the 2–12 times tables with factors from 1 through 12. A round may include facts such as 4 × 7, 9 × 6, 11 × 8, or 12 × 12.

    For Teachers

    This mixed practice game is designed for the stage after students have already worked with individual times tables. It can be used as a quick fluency check, a math-center activity, or a short review at the start of class.

    Why the Wrong Facts Return

    When Check is pressed, wrong and unanswered facts are carried into the next round. Students can freely arrange answers before submitting, then get a clear result and a focused set of facts to revisit.

    Suggested Use

    • Use one or two 10-question rounds as a warm-up
    • Ask students to notice which facts keep returning
    • Return to an individual times table when a pattern of difficulty appears

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How many questions are in each round?

    Each round contains 10 mixed multiplication facts.

    What happens when a student gets a fact wrong?

    The game does not correct answers while the student is working. When Check is pressed, a wrong answer is shown with the correct answer and that fact returns in the next round.

    When does a missed fact stop repeating?

    When the fact returns in a later round and is correct when that round is checked, it leaves the review pool.

    Is the game timed?

    No. The goal is accurate recall and useful repetition, not speed pressure.

    Need to Review One Table?

    Mixed practice is most useful after students know the individual tables. If one group of facts is still difficult, go back and practice that table on its own.
