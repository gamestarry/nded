The 10 times table is one of the clearest multiplication patterns. Each product is ten groups of a number, so the answers grow by 10 every time.

Learn the 10 Times Table

To multiply a whole number by 10, shift it one place to the left in base ten. For basic facts, students often recognize this as adding a zero to the end of the number.

Use the Place-Value Pattern

Example:

10 × 7 = 70

Why Learning the 10 Times Table Matters

  • Strengthen place-value understanding
  • Build fast recall for multiples of 10
  • Prepare for 11s and 12s using known facts

Practice Tips

  • Say the equation aloud before moving the answer
  • Use a known fact to work out a fact you forget
  • Keep practice short and repeat it regularly

Frequently Asked Questions

What facts are practiced in the 10 Times Table Game?

This level practices 10 × 1 through 10 × 10. The chart also shows the complete 10 times table through 10 × 12.

Why does the game use two rounds?

The first round makes the number pattern visible. The second round shuffles the facts so students practice recall rather than relying only on order.

How long should a child practice?

Short, regular practice is usually more useful than a long drill session. Five to ten focused minutes is enough for many students.

Can teachers use this in class?

Yes. It works for independent practice, math centers, warm-ups, or a quick review with a small group.

For Teachers

The 10s are a useful confidence-building bridge from single-digit tables into extended multiplication facts.

  • Use the ordered round to discuss patterns and strategies
  • Use the shuffled round to check fact recall
  • Encourage students to explain the strategy they used for a difficult fact
